# PORTFOLIO — WEBSITE COPY BRIEF

## Project Overview

Portfolio is a sailing vessel and artist-run floating gallery currently sailing the Mediterranean. It functions simultaneously as a community gathering space, artist residency, and traveling exhibition dedicated to site-specific, temporary, grassroots art. The boat moves port to port, collaborating with local artists at each stop — offering the vessel as gallery space and opening it to the public free of charge.

The project was initiated by Ketil (captain, Norwegian urban planner and lifelong sailor) and Hala (American-Egyptian visual artist, MFA). The boat and the dream of sailing the Mediterranean belongs to Ketil. Portfolio as an artist initiative is Hala's vision. Together they offer the safe security of a vessel held within the open opportunity of an artist space.

The name Portfolio carries its own etymology — *portare* (to carry) and *foglio* (the leaf, the page). A vessel that carries leaves of work across water.

---

## Tone & Voice

The language of this site is poetic, grounded, warm and unhurried. It should feel like the project itself — moving at the speed of wind, not algorithm. Avoid founder language, startup culture, institutional formality, or anything overly trendy. The voice is that of artists speaking to artists and curious strangers alike. It is inclusive, borderless, dreamy but specific. Whimsical but never precious.

Key throughlines that appear across the site —
- *the harbor is our gallery. the sea is your studio* — the site tagline
- *we move at the speed of the wind* — lives on The Initiative page, do not repeat elsewhere
- *find us in the harbor* — the footer closing line, appears at the bottom of every page
- *come find us in the harbor* — closes The Initiative page specifically
- the sea, the horizon, the tide, the wind — these are not decorative metaphors, they are the actual context of the project and should be treated as such throughout

### 1. THE INITIATIVE

This is the landing page. It holds the vision and spirit of the project. It opens and closes in italics — the italic lines function as the dream, the roman text as the body of it.

---

*Portfolio is a sailboat and an idea.*

There is a kind of making that requires distance from the familiar. A stepping away from the studio, the city, the known — toward something more open, more uncertain, more alive.

Portfolio is a floating gallery moving port to port across the Mediterranean, crewed by artists, open to strangers, guided by wind. At each harbor we drop anchor, open our doors, and invite the local art community aboard — to show work, to gather, to make something that only this place, at this moment, could have made.

Portfolio is a collective, a residency, a traveling exhibition space. It is also something harder to name — a threshold, a chorus, a small act of faith in what becomes possible when creative people find each other at the water's edge.

Each show exists once, fully, then the tide carries us forward.

The sea is our context and our anchor. The horizon, our reference point. We move at the speed of the wind.

*Come find us in the harbor.*

### 2. FOR ARTISTS

This page is more intimate than The Initiative — warmer, like a letter. It introduces the two ways to participate — The Exhibition and The Residency — while keeping the poetic tone intact. It closes with three short italic lines.

---

*This is an invitation before it is an application.*

Making on water is its own kind of practice. Without the familiar walls of the studio, without the gravity of your usual surroundings, something shifts. The horizon becomes your reference. The light changes every hour. The boat moves beneath you, and somehow, so does the work.

Portfolio welcomes artists who are curious about what that shift might open up — painters, sculptors, photographers, performers, sound artists, installation makers, and those who haven't yet found the word for what they do.

There are two ways to come aboard —

**The Exhibition**
In each port we collaborate with local artists to create a site-specific show, opening the boat as a public gallery space. The work lives here temporarily, fully, and then the tide moves on. If you are based in one of our ports of call and feel a pull toward making something for this particular threshold — the water, the vessel, the transient gathering — we would love to hear from you.

**The Residency**
For those who want longer, slower time — there is space aboard Portfolio while she is in transit. Days at sea. Mornings with no destination yet arrived at. Time to contemplate, to sketch, to sit with a project that needs open sky more than it needs answers.

This is not a program with prescribed outcomes. It is an invitation to slow down, look outward, and let the work find its own shape.

As we arrive into port, each resident is invited to share — an opening, a showing, a moment of gathering before the tide moves on again. Not a finished work necessarily, but an honest one. Something made from the crossing. The harbor becomes your audience and your witness.

*The passage is the studio. The arrival is the gift.*

*We are not looking for finished ideas. We are looking for open ones.*

*Come as you are. Bring your curiosity. The sea will do the rest.*

### 4. THE CREW

This page introduces Ketil and Hala. It opens and closes with italic framing lines. Each person has their own section separated by a horizontal rule. The closing lines bring them together as a pair.

---

*Every vessel needs people who know how to hold a line and when to let one go.*

Portfolio is carried by two — Ketil and Hala.

---

Ketil grew up on a farm in Norway, two hours from Oslo, where winters meant felling trees and splitting wood by hand and summers meant building, repairing, tending. He learned early that things worth having require care and patience. That good work is done slowly, and with your whole self.

He has spent his career as an urban planner thinking about public space at the largest scale — how cities breathe, how humans and nature find their balance, how communities are shaped by the spaces held between buildings, between people. He dreams of floating cities. Of human life unmoored from fixed ground, finding new ways to gather and belong.

He has sailed since childhood — across the Caribbean, the North Sea, the North Pacific and the Mediterranean. The sea has always been his truest compass. When the opportunity came to take a year and follow that compass fully, he did not hesitate. He pointed the bow south toward the Mediterranean — the ancient sea of Homer's great stories, the water that held Odysseus, that carried civilizations toward one another across millennia — and he went.

This boat was Ketil's dream. He holds it with steady hands and an open heart. He is calm by nature, level headed, quietly abundant. The kind of person who finds the best perspective in any situation and offers it to everyone around him without fanfare.

He believes in Dugnad — the Norwegian tradition of communal labor, showing up to help build something together simply because you are part of the community.

Portfolio is his Dugnad, offered to the wider world.

---

Hala is a visual artist whose practice lives at the intersection of time, human connection and the dissolution of walls between people. She thinks deeply about what it means to be alive in a body, reaching toward other living things with the limited senses and time we are given. Her work — spanning ceramics, glass, performance, documentation, fiber, metal and participatory forms — is rooted in a single enduring question: *how do we connect more authentically across the distance between us?*

Borderlessness is both her artistic theme and her way of moving through the world.

She has spent years collecting, documenting, gathering — building bodies of work that serve as markers of shared human passage. She understands the temporary not as loss but as the very thing that makes a moment matter.

The boat was Ketil's dream. Portfolio was Hala's answer.

---

*Together they offer something rare —*
*the safe security of a vessel, held within the open opportunity of an artist space.*

*They are not curators in the traditional sense.*
*They are hosts. Collaborators. Fellow travelers.*

*Come aboard.*

**What a Residency Looks Like**

You will live aboard Portfolio as a full crew member, sailing between ports while developing work in direct response to the vessel, the sea, and the communities we encounter along the way.

Days at sea are yours. We ask only that you engage with the journey — the light, the movement, the people we meet — and that you bring something to share with the public upon arrival into port. Not a finished work necessarily, but an honest one. Something made from the crossing.

The length of your residency is something we determine together. Tell us what you need. We are listening.

*The passage is the studio. The arrival is the gift.*

---

*[ image — detail shot, hand on a line or light through a sail ]*

---

**Life Aboard**

A private cabin for the duration of your residency. Shared communal spaces include the saloon, galley, and open deck — all of which are available as studio and exhibition space. The boat is small. The horizon is not.

Artists are encouraged to work with found, natural, or locally sourced materials that respond to the places we visit. We cannot support large scale production. We can support large scale thinking.

---

*[ image — people gathered on deck at dusk, faces not fully visible ]*

---

**Community**

Portfolio opens its decks at every port — to locals, to wanderers, to anyone drawn to the water. As a resident artist you are invited to engage directly with the communities we encounter. To run conversations, lead workshops, share your process. The boat becomes a gathering space. You become part of that gathering.

---

**Who Should Come Aboard**

We are not looking for finished ideas. We are looking for open ones.

*Art is not a medium. It is a way of living.*

Portfolio welcomes anyone who makes — painters, sculptors, installation artists, performers, photographers, sound artists, writers, poets, ceramicists, chefs, experience makers, architects of atmosphere, and those who haven't yet found the word for what they do. You do not need sailing experience. You need only a willingness to let the sea change you a little.

We particularly welcome artists whose voices are underrepresented in mainstream gallery and institutional spaces. This project exists outside those walls by design.

*These are letters from the water.*

Part captain's log, part field notes. Part record, part reverie. Not a blog. Not a newsletter. Just notes — written when something needed to be said. From the deck at dawn. From a harbor we didn't expect to love. From the middle of the sea with no land in any direction.

Ketil and Hala write here. Residents write here. Written when something needed to be said and the words arrived before the moment passed. Sometimes the sea seems to write here itself.
